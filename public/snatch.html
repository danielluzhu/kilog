Lu Xiaojun — 176 kg snatch

176 kg snatched
81 kg bodyweight class
2.17× bodyweight ratio

What each row shows

  • First pullrow 1, frames 1–4

    Bar breaks off the platform with the back angle held constant — hips and shoulders rise together, chest stays over the bar, arms long and relaxed. The bar tracks back toward him as the knees clear out of the way.

  • Transition & second pullrow 1, frames 5–6

    Knees re-bend under the bar (the double knee bend), the torso swings to vertical, and he drives through the floor into full extension of hips, knees, and ankles. This is the only moment in the lift where real upward force gets put into the bar.

  • Pull under & turnoverrow 2, frames 7–11

    The arms finally bend, but to pull himself down rather than the bar up. Feet come off the platform and reset wider, the elbows whip around, and the bar rotates from hip height to overhead while he drops beneath it.

  • Receiving positionrow 2–3, frames 12–14

    He catches it in a full overhead squat: arms locked, bar stacked over the mid-foot and slightly behind the ears, torso upright, hips well below the knees. Nothing here is a strength move — it is mobility and position holding a load he cannot press.

  • Recoveryrow 3, frames 15–17

    Stand up out of the bottom without letting the bar drift forward or the elbows soften. The overhead squat strength that finishes the lift is why it is trained as an accessory in its own right.

  • Down signalrow 3, frame 18

    Motionless, arms and legs extended, feet in line — three white lights, and then the celebration.

Why it is worth staring at

The snatch is the most technically demanding lift in the log — a barbell moved from the floor to overhead in one continuous motion, in well under a second. It classifies as a Complex lift in the Exercise Dictionary, which is exactly the category Prilepin's Table was written for: low reps per set, moderate total volume, because quality of position degrades long before the muscles do.
