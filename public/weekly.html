Six training days and one rest day, built around the snatch and the clean & jerk, with the weighted pull-up and weighted dip second. The week leads heavy three days running — snatch and front squat Monday, pull-up and dip Tuesday, clean & jerk Wednesday — then Thursday drops to a short run and bodybuilding, Friday runs all three Olympic lifts at medium, and Saturday takes a heavy squat with the pulls and the week's volume straight into Sunday off. Why the week is shaped this way

  • Each goal lift owns a day. The snatch has Monday, the pull-up and dip have Tuesday, the clean & jerk has Wednesday, the squat has Saturday. Nothing heavy shares a day with something else heavy except the front squat, which rides along with the snatch so the legs take one hard session instead of two.
  • Three heavy days back to back, then a release valve. Monday through Wednesday are the top-end days; Thursday is a short run and isolation work and costs less than half of any of them. That's what makes six straight training days work — the middle of the week is where the recovery is, not the end.
  • Friday is the technique day, at medium. All three Olympic lifts in one session at a weight that can be made every rep. If Friday and Saturday start running into each other, the jerk is the first thing to cut — it's already inside the clean & jerk above it.
  • The squat carries two heavy days. Monday's front squat and Saturday's back squat, with the pause front squat medium in between. That's more, and harder, than any other movement gets — the trade is that the snatch and the clean & jerk run on two exposures each instead of three. Worth watching: if the Monday snatch starts feeling heavy off the floor, Saturday's squat is the thing to take down first.
  • Volume is banked in front of the rest day. Saturday is the heaviest legs of the week and it lands the day before Sunday off, rather than in the middle where it would have to be paid for twice.
  • The run stays short and easy. 25–35 minutes midweek, on the day nothing else is chasing a number. Aerobic base costs nothing if it stays aerobic.
  • The week type waves across a month. Heavy → medium → heavy → deload keeps average weekly load near what you've actually been absorbing, instead of running the top week every week.

Customizing this page

Customize turns every day into an editable form: change the sets, reps and intensity band, swap an exercise for any abbreviation in the dictionary, drop a slot, add a slot, add a day or make a day a rest day. Edits save to this browser as you make them and survive a reload — Reset to default throws them away. Copy as JSON and Paste JSON move a plan between devices.

Each slot's intensity is a percentage band of an estimated 1RM. By default that's the exercise's own best, but a slot can be pegged to a different lift — the snatch high pull is written as a percentage of the snatch, the pause front squat as a percentage of the front squat — so the accessory follows the lift it serves. Bodyweight movements are prescribed as added weight (+41 kg), matching how they're logged.

To change the default that Reset restores, edit DEFAULT_PLAN at the top of public/weekly.js.
