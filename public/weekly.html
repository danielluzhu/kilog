Why the week is shaped this way
  • Each goal lift owns a day. The snatch has Monday, the pull-up and dip have Tuesday, the clean & jerk has Wednesday, the back squat has Friday. The one day running two heavy lifts is Monday — snatch first, front squat second — because the squat is the thing on that day that can afford to go second.
  • Three heavy days back to back, then a release valve. Monday through Wednesday are the top-end days; Thursday is isolation work and costs less than half of any of them. That's what makes six straight training days work — the middle of the week is where the recovery is, not the end.
  • Tuesday costs the legs nothing. It is a heavy day in fatigue units and an off day for everything the snatch and the clean draw on, which is why Wednesday's heavy clean runs on Monday's legs at 48 hours rather than on anything Tuesday did. The same goes for Thursday. Read the leg load as on, off, on, off, on, on, rest.
  • Friday is the technique day, and the heavy squat. All three Olympic lifts at a weight that can be made every rep, then the week's top set of back squats behind them. If the squat is what's suffering, the jerk is the first thing to cut — it's already inside the clean & jerk above it.
  • The squat carries two heavy days. Monday's front squat and Friday's back squat, with Wednesday's pause front squat medium between them — three squat days, two days apart each, and none on the weekend. That's more, and harder, than any other movement gets: the trade is that the snatch and the clean & jerk run on two exposures each instead of three. The longest gap in the rotation is the one that spans the rest day, Friday's back squat to Monday's front squat.
  • Volume is banked in front of the rest day. Friday's squat and Saturday's pulls are the heaviest legs of the week, and they land against Sunday off rather than in the middle where they would have to be paid for twice.
  • The run stays short and easy. 25–35 minutes on Saturday, behind the pulls and in front of the rest day. Aerobic base costs nothing if it stays aerobic.
  • The week type waves across a month. Heavy → medium → heavy → deload keeps average weekly load near what you've actually been absorbing, instead of running the top week every week.
Customizing this page

Customize turns every day into an editable form: change the sets, reps and intensity band, swap an exercise for any abbreviation in the dictionary, drop a slot, add a slot, add a day or make a day a rest day. Edits save to this browser as you make them and survive a reload — Reset to default throws them away. Copy as JSON and Paste JSON move a plan between devices.

Each slot's intensity is a percentage band of an estimated 1RM. By default that's the exercise's own best, but a slot can be pegged to a different lift — the snatch high pull is written as a percentage of the snatch, the pause front squat as a percentage of the front squat — so the accessory follows the lift it serves. Bodyweight movements are prescribed as added weight (+41 kg), matching how they're logged.

To change the default that Reset restores, edit DEFAULT_PLAN at the top of public/weekly.js.
